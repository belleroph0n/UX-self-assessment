# v1.0.0 - first public release

First public release of the **User experience self assessment**: a free resource that helps product and delivery teams check the quality of their application's user experience against NZ Government standards and international good practice.

**Live site:** https://belleroph0n.github.io/UX-self-assessment/

## What it does

- **Two assessments:** Simple (19 questions, 10-15 minutes, recommended) or Detailed (96 questions, with evidence to look for)
- **Service profile:** six questions that set questions that don't apply to N/A automatically
- **UX health score** out of 100, rated Strong (85+), Good (70-84), Developing (50-69) or Priority focus (below 50). If any critical criteria are answered "Not yet", the score is capped at 60 until they're in place.
- **Materiality** rated 1 (Immaterial) to 5 (Critical) from 10 factors, reported separately from the score, with a suggested service tier (Platinum, Gold, Silver, Bronze) and its target
- **Report** with colour-coded results, what's working well, where to focus first (with supporting links), results by category, and every improvement opportunity with your notes
- **Privacy by design:** answers are saved only in the browser and can be downloaded and reopened as a file. Nothing is sent anywhere.

## Question set

Question set **v1.52**: 96 criteria across 10 categories (accessibility; usability and design; content and tone of voice; brand and visual consistency; security and privacy; reliability, observability and service levels; support and contact channels; legal and regulatory; content management and governance; measurement and continuous improvement). See [CHANGELOG.md](https://github.com/belleroph0n/UX-self-assessment/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md) for how it was developed and [content/sources.yaml](https://github.com/belleroph0n/UX-self-assessment/blob/main/content/sources.yaml) for the 54 sources.

## Changes since the first publish (24 Oct 2026)

- New site name, home page introduction and tab titles
- Supportive wording throughout: "Not yet" answers, journey-based rating labels, critical criteria rather than failures, and a "What's working well" section
- Linked references on the accessibility page; full-width service tiers table
- GitHub Actions updated to current versions (Node.js 24)

## Quality checks

- Scoring tests reproduce the original criteria workbook's results exactly
- Every page passes automated WCAG 2.2 AA checks (Axe) on desktop and mobile
- Supporting links are checked on every change and weekly

## Known limitations

- Service tier definitions are proposals and haven't been agreed
- Manual keyboard and screen reader testing is still to be done
- digital.gov.au links are excluded from automated link checks (the site blocks them) and need checking by hand
